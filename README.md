      <p>We built this brand because "closest" isn't the same as "right." Every shade we carry was mixed against real skin, in real light, on people who kept telling us the last bottle was almost — but not quite.</p>

    <h2>Blog excerpt — "Why 'just tired' isn't a diagnosis"</h2>
    <p class="brief">Brief: an SEO-minded wellness article that needed real clinical grounding without sounding like a textbook. Drafted using a nursing background for accuracy.</p>
    <div class="copy-block">
      <p>"I'm just tired" is one of the most common things people say to their doctor, and one of the least useful — not because it isn't true, but because tiredness isn't a single thing with a single cause.</p>
      <p>Clinically, fatigue gets sorted into rough buckets: the kind sleep fixes, the kind that points to something metabolic (thyroid, iron, blood sugar), the kind tied to mood or stress load, and the kind that's a side effect of a medication doing exactly what it's supposed to do, just more than you'd like.</p>
      <p>The distinction matters because the fix is different for each one. More sleep won't touch an iron deficiency. A vitamin won't fix a thyroid that's underperforming. And no amount of willpower substitutes for a nervous system that's been in low-grade alarm mode for six months.</p>
      <p>So instead of "I'm tired," try bringing your doctor a pattern: when it's worse, what makes it better, and what else changed around the time it started. That's the sentence that actually gets you somewhere.</p>
    </div>
    <span class="tag">Goal: earn trust through precision, not jargon</span>
  </section>

    <h2>Essay excerpt — on being the "reliable one"</h2>
    <p class="brief">Brief: a Substack-style personal essay opening, voice-driven and unpolished on purpose.</p>
    <div class="copy-block">
      <p>Somewhere along the way I became the person people call when something breaks — a plan, a deadline, a family arrangement no one else wants to manage. I used to think this was a compliment. Lately I think it might just be a job nobody applied me for.</p>
      <p>There's a specific kind of tired that comes from being competent in public. It doesn't look like burnout from the outside. It looks like someone who "has it together," which is exactly the problem — the more together you look, the less anyone thinks to ask if you're carrying too much.</p>
      <p>I'm not writing this because I've solved it. I'm writing it because I noticed, this week, that I hadn't asked anyone for help in longer than I could comfortably admit.</p>
